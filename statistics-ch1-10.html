Statistics for physicists: chapters 1 to 10

Prof. Ackland’s notes (Fourier Analysis and Statistics, PHYS09055). The whole course rests on one idea: data are drawn from a probability distribution that your physical model predicts, and statistics checks whether the data look like a plausible draw.

  • Model the data
  • Predict, exactly or by simulation
  • Pick a statistic T(D)
  • Compare with simulated T
  • Estimate parameters
  • State what was fitted

Binomial to Gaussian (chapters 3 and 4)

Bars are the binomial distribution; the line is the Gaussian with the same mean and variance. Push n up and watch them merge. Keep p tiny and n large to see the Poisson regime.
